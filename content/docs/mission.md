# Mission

## The Question Behind the Project

Crypto was supposed to end a specific injustice. The first blockchain was a
response to a banking crisis, built on one charge: the financial system
privatizes its gains and socializes its losses. Remove the intermediary, the idea
went, and you remove its power to extract.

The technology delivered that open, neutral network. What got built on top of it
did not. The same infrastructure turned out to be an ideal venue for the very
extraction it was meant to replace — now faster, and sold as liberation. The
people the system was supposed to free became the liquidity it consumed.

So the project begins with an uncomfortable question: **why does the
decentralized world, which promised equality, end up looking so much like the old
financial system it set out to replace?**

## What We Are Building

**Regarded Games is the market that technology was meant to make possible** — one
whose rules are written, enforced, and owned by the people who play in it.

Most markets are decided by two advantages ordinary players never have: **better
information** and **deeper pockets**. Insiders see the order flow first and hold
enough capital to move prices. That isn't bad luck or a skill gap; it is the
structure. The house doesn't cheat against the rules — the house *is* the rules.

We remove those two advantages from the structure itself. Every state is public,
every rule is enforced by code rather than discretion, and no operator can reach
in and tilt the outcome. Smart money is welcome to play; it simply cannot cheat.
On a field where the structural edge is gone, the only things left that decide a
game are the quality of your strategy and the strength of your coordination.

## Why It's a Game

We make it a game because a game is the most honest way to make the structure
visible. **Class War: The Game** stages the contradiction at the center of crypto
— open and equal in who may enter, sharply concentrated in who prevails — as the
explicit object of play. Two classes, the Capitalists who pull wealth into few
hands and the Proletarians who spread it back out, fight over a real prize pool on
a field where everyone can see everything.

But the game is the *vehicle*, not the point. The point is what players discover
by trading in a market where the cheats are gone: that on a level field, your own
best outcome is usually bound up with the collective's. The lesson lands not as a
sermon but as a felt, profit-driven experience — players reach cooperation through
arithmetic, because the numbers, laid out in the open, point there.

## From Owning Coins to Coordinating Capital

The dominant story about crypto treats it as a way to *hold* value outside the
banking system — money you own and store yourself. That was a real breakthrough,
but it stops at simple ownership.

We think the higher use is **coordination**. Smart contracts can do more than hold
a balance; they can run a whole market with no middleman skimming the value that
everyone's activity creates. Remove that layer and the surplus flows straight back
to the participants who generated it — and so do the rules. Regarded Games is the
practical application of that shift: a market people actually trade in, whose
surplus and whose rules both belong to the people in it.

→ Read more: [From Owning Coins to Coordinating Capital](/whitepaper/part-i-the-philosophical-foundation#52-from-owning-coins-to-coordinating-capital)

## The Experiment

Underneath the game is a claim we intend to prove, with real money rather than
rhetoric: that decentralized governance can produce genuine fairness and
participation, and that handing ordinary people the tools to rebuild the economy
on fairer terms is what this technology was always for.

The first game mode is only the baseline. Because the players own the rules, the
fair field is something they can keep reconfiguring — the DAO can add new modes
that test coordination in new ways, from seasons backed by volatile real assets to
ones that start everyone on perfectly equal footing. The market is a war, but for
the first time the weapons are distributed equally, and the rules belong to the
people holding them.
